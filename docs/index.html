What are the nine pass types, and how was this made?

Method. 4.5 million open-play passes (2013/14 to 25 Oct 2026) were clustered with k-means into nine types using where each pass starts, how far it goes toward goal and sideways, and whether it was lofted or a cross. Each player-season is then his share of passes in each type. Players are grouped within position with k-means on those shares, with a separate pass cutoff and number of groups for each position, chosen by split-half reliability, silhouette and stability checks. Types (4, 3 and 4 groups) are the stable level; styles (7, 5 and 6 groups) are finer but their borders wobble, so treat them as neighbourhoods on a continuum. Names are the author's reading of each group's most-used passes.

Caveats. Types form a continuum, not islands (silhouette about 0.2). Position is the one a player most often started in. Pass xT (value created by completed passes) ignores carrying, defending and off-ball work; xT before 2024/25 uses a grid fitted on the same data, so compare within a season. The 2026/27 season is only 8 matches per team old: players with 200+ passes are shown (full seasons use a cutoff of 200 for defenders and 350 for midfielders and attackers), so groups may still move. Data: WhoScored event data.
